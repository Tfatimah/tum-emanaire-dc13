What if AI changes not only the answers we find, but the questions we learn to ask?
02
The observation

A generation accustomed to creating digitally.

Across spring and winter intakes, I have mentored, trained and worked alongside university interns and fresh graduates.

From interactive media and digital classrooms to AI-assisted creative tools, some have become comfortable starting with an imagined result and exploring ways to achieve it. Their practices invite an empirical question—not a universal claim about Gen Z.

03
The conceptual shift

Two possible pathways to professional judgement

Conceptual pathways, not competing generations or proven learning models.

Knowledge-led pathway
01 Acquire knowledge
02 Practise through experience
03 Apply established methods
04 Develop judgement
AI-assisted exploration pathway
01 Imagine an outcome
02 Explore possibilities with AI
03 Introduce constraints and test alternatives
04 Reframe and refine judgement
The key question: does AI-assisted dialogue develop the person's problem-framing capability—or primarily improve the immediate output?
04
The human–AI exploration loop

How thinking develops through dialogue

Professional reasoning is rarely a single prompt. A person sets goals, explores suggestions, tests constraints, evaluates alternatives and revises the framing.

Select a step to see which human capability the research could observe.

Articulating the problem, desired outcome, assumptions and boundaries before asking AI for possibilities.

05
Research questions

Three questions. One underlying curiosity.

01

Problem framing

How do young professionals use AI to frame and reframe unfamiliar workplace problems?

02

Shared judgement

How do they compare alternatives, evaluate AI suggestions and decide which cognitive responsibilities to retain?

03

Skill development

How do these practices develop over time, and can capabilities transfer beyond the immediate AI-assisted task?

06
A possible research approach

From lived practices to observable evidence

An exploratory, potentially mixed-method design to be refined with Prof. Sandra Cortesi and the supervisory team.

01

Listen

Interviews and real-world task walkthroughs to understand existing approaches without presupposing deficits.

02

Observe

Participatory workplace scenarios to examine framing, evaluation, appropriate delegation and judgement.

03

Revisit

Repeated tasks or follow-ups to study development and test whether reasoning transfers to unfamiliar situations.

Participants' perspectives, consent, privacy and secure handling of workplace data would inform the design.

09
About the researcher

Enterprise practice meets human-centred research

Tasneem Fatima · AI product and workflow practitioner

Studying how people learn, reason and make decisions with intelligent systems.

I bring 12+ years across enterprise technology, product management, organisational transformation, AI adoption and workflow design. My experience includes mentoring interns and newly graduated professionals, conducting user research and investigating real human–AI collaboration.

My independent exploratory project When AI Thinks With Us reflects my transition from practitioner observations toward systematic research.

Motivation and connection

As a first-generation university graduate whose educational journey was largely self-directed, I value how guidance, opportunity and independent judgement shape people's development. My mentoring work and professional experience inform this curiosity.

Prof. Sandra Cortesi's research on young people's digital participation inspires an approach that listens to young professionals' actual experiences and allows their perspectives to shape the inquiry.
